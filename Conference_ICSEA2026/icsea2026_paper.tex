\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) improves large language model (LLM)
reliability by grounding responses in external knowledge, yet RAG architectures
inherently trust their retrieval output, creating a \emph{Security--Reliability
Gap}: high semantic relevance does not guarantee factual truth. Adversaries
exploit this through \emph{knowledge poisoning}---injecting malicious documents
into the corpus to induce targeted misinformation. We propose an autonomous
\emph{Evaluation Agent} that operates as defensive middleware in the RAG
pipeline, combining Natural Language Inference (NLI) for factual verification, a
five-signal poison detector with relevance-weighted aggregation, and a composite
\emph{Trust Index} $T = 0.4\,F + 0.35\,C + 0.25\,(1-P)$ augmented with a
non-linear dampener for high-contamination contexts. On TruthfulQA with
Llama~3.3~70B, the agent reaches 91\% accuracy and 100\% precision (a +7\%
improvement over a naive always-trust baseline); instruction injection is
detected at 100\% recall, whereas entity-swap and subtle in-place edits remain
near-undetectable---an architectural limit of surface-signal detection. A
study across \emph{three} LLMs shows the Trust Index is LLM-dependent, and that
generation \emph{style} matters more than size: a 7B model (87\% accuracy)
outperforms a 35B model (69\%) whose hedged outputs trigger systematic false
positives. The score stays discriminative for every model (ROC-AUC 0.73--0.81),
and lightweight per-LLM threshold calibration restores baseline-competitive
accuracy. Results are stable across repeated runs ($90.6{\pm}0.5\%$ over five),
and detection is retrieval-depth-invariant ($K{=}3 \equiv K{=}5$). We release the framework, attack generator, and
reproducible experiments.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%==============================================================================
Large language models (LLMs) built on the Transformer architecture
\cite{vaswani2017attention} have become foundational engines for
knowledge-intensive applications, but their parametric knowledge is frozen at
training time and they are prone to \emph{hallucination}---fluent output that is
factually incorrect \cite{ji2023survey}. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
\cite{lewis2020rag} mitigates both problems by retrieving relevant documents
from an external corpus and conditioning generation on them.

This grounding introduces a new attack surface: the integrity of the retrieved
data itself. Standard RAG assumes the knowledge base is benign, but in
open-world deployments adversaries can inject malicious documents through
\emph{knowledge poisoning} \cite{zou2024poisoned}. Such documents are crafted to
maximize retrieval similarity with target queries; once retrieved, they steer
the LLM toward attacker-chosen misinformation. Poisoning fewer than 0.1\% of a
corpus can yield attack success rates above 40\% \cite{zou2024poisoned}.

We term the underlying problem the \textbf{Security--Reliability Gap}: RAG
systems verify neither the truthfulness nor the safety of what they retrieve,
treating high semantic relevance as a proxy for truth. Existing evaluation
frameworks such as RAGAS \cite{es2024ragas} and ARES \cite{saadfalcon2024ares}
measure \emph{faithfulness to context} but not the \emph{integrity of the
context}. A RAG system that faithfully answers from a poisoned document scores
well under these metrics while emitting compromised output. Moreover, most
poisoning defenses are offline corpus-cleaning steps; there is no online,
autonomous trust layer that operates inside the inference loop.

This paper presents an \emph{Evaluation Agent} that fills this gap. Its
contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The Trust Index}, a unified security--reliability metric that
  combines factuality, cross-document consistency, and poison safety, augmented
  with a non-linear dampener that prevents high factuality from masking
  contamination.
  \item A \textbf{multi-signal poison detector} (linguistic, structural,
  intra-document, cross-document, and semantic-outlier signals) with
  relevance-weighted aggregation.
  \item An \textbf{empirical per-strategy characterization} of detection
  difficulty across four poisoning strategies, reported with bootstrap
  confidence intervals, revealing the hierarchy injection $\gg$ contradiction
  $\gg$ subtle $\approx$ entity-swap.
  \item Evidence that \textbf{NLI-based trust scoring is LLM-dependent} across
  \textbf{three LLMs}---governed by generation style, not model size---with a
  threshold-independent (ROC) analysis and a \textbf{per-LLM calibration}
  procedure in which each model requires a distinct decision threshold.
  \item A \textbf{retrieval-depth-invariance} result ($K{=}3 \equiv K{=}5$)
  showing the recall ceiling is set by attack difficulty, not retrieval breadth.
  \item A \textbf{reproducible open implementation} with an adversarial dataset
  generator and serialized experiment artifacts.
\end{enumerate}

%==============================================================================
\section{Background and Related Work}
%==============================================================================
\textbf{RAG architectures.} RAG has evolved from naive retrieve-then-generate
pipelines to modular and agentic systems \cite{gao2024retrieval}. Modular RAG
treats retriever, generator, and evaluator as interchangeable components;
Self-RAG \cite{asai2024selfrag} adds ``critic tokens'' for self-reflection,
though it is unclear whether such self-correction is robust against adversarial
inputs designed to manipulate the critic.

\textbf{Evaluation.} Retrieval quality is measured with IR metrics such as
Recall@$k$ and MRR \cite{karpukhin2020dense}, which capture relevance but not
safety: a poisoned document engineered to be highly relevant scores perfectly.
Generation quality has moved from n-gram overlap to factuality-oriented
measures. TruthfulQA \cite{lin2022truthfulqa} targets imitative falsehoods, and
the TRUE benchmark \cite{honovich2022true} standardizes factual-consistency
evaluation using NLI. RAGAS \cite{es2024ragas} and ARES \cite{saadfalcon2024ares}
use LLMs as judges of faithfulness and relevance but do not assess corpus
integrity.

\textbf{Attacks and defenses.} Indirect prompt injection embeds malicious
instructions into retrievable content \cite{greshake2023indirect}. PoisonedRAG
\cite{zou2024poisoned} formalizes knowledge poisoning of the inference corpus.
On the defense side, NLI-based verification treats a retrieved document as
premise and the answer as hypothesis to quantify consistency
\cite{shuster2021retrieval}. Our work differs by unifying factual verification
\emph{and} poison detection into a single interpretable score that operates
online, and by characterizing where such surface-signal detection succeeds and
fails.

the final trust decision (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). It orchestrates three modules:
an NLI verifier, a poison detector, and a Trust Index calculator, and emits a
numeric trust score, a categorical trust level, and human-readable warnings.

\subsection{Factual Verification via NLI}
The NLI verifier uses \texttt{facebook/bart-large-mnli} as a sequence
classifier. For each retrieved document (premise) and the generated answer
(hypothesis) it produces entailment, neutral, and contradiction probabilities.
The factuality score $S_{\text{factuality}}$ aggregates entailment over
documents with a meaningful entailment signal; when none exists, an inconclusive
baseline of $0.5$ is returned. This entailment-focused design is motivated by an
empirical observation: \texttt{bart-large-mnli} assigns near-maximal
contradiction ($\approx0.99$) to \emph{both} genuine contradictions and merely
unrelated text, so using raw contradiction as a negative signal would
systematically penalize off-topic but benign documents. Strong negative signals
are therefore reserved for the dedicated poison-detection pathway.

\subsection{Multi-Signal Poison Detection}
The poison detector combines five independent signals per document:
(1)~\emph{linguistic patterns} (instruction-override phrases, contradiction
markers); (2)~\emph{structural anomalies} (excessive uppercase, suspicious
repetition); (3)~\emph{intra-document consistency} (NLI between the first and
second halves of a document); (4)~\emph{cross-document consistency} (pairwise
contradiction checks, scoped so a single poisoned document cannot inflate the
poison probability of clean neighbors); and (5)~\emph{semantic-outlier analysis}
(embedding deviation from the batch centroid). When retrieval scores are
available, document-level probabilities are combined with relevance weighting,
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{overall}} = \sum_{i=1}^{k} w_i\,P_i,
\qquad w_i = \frac{s_i}{\sum_j s_j},
\end{equation}
where $s_i$ is the retrieval similarity of document $i$. This prevents
low-relevance suspicious neighbors from dominating the batch verdict.

\subsection{The Trust Index}
The Trust Index $T\in[0,1]$ is a weighted combination of three components:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:trust}
T = \alpha\,S_{\text{factuality}} + \beta\,S_{\text{consistency}}
    + \gamma\,(1 - P_{\text{poison}}),
\end{equation}
with default weights $\alpha{=}0.40$, $\beta{=}0.35$, $\gamma{=}0.25$
($\alpha{+}\beta{+}\gamma{=}1$). The weights reflect signal reliability:
NLI entailment (factuality) is the most precise, cross-document agreement
(consistency) a strong corpus-integrity proxy, and heuristic poison signals are
weighted lowest to limit false-positive influence.

\textbf{Non-linear dampener.} A purely linear $T$ fails under high
contamination: if the LLM ignores a poisoned passage and answers from the
legitimate part, factuality and consistency stay high while $P_{\text{poison}}$
is large. Because $\gamma{=}0.25$, the poison term can reduce $T$ by at most
$0.25$, so $T$ can exceed the threshold $\tau{=}0.5$ despite $P_{\text{poison}}
{=}0.9$. We therefore apply a multiplicative dampener when
$P_{\text{poison}}>0.70$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:damp}
d(P) =
\begin{cases}
1.0 & P \le 0.70\\[2pt]
1.0 - 0.4\,\dfrac{P-0.70}{0.30} & P > 0.70
\end{cases}
\qquad T_{\text{final}} = T\cdot d(P).
\end{equation}
The dampener is continuous at the threshold ($d(0.70){=}1$), bounded
($d(1.0){=}0.6$, i.e.\ at most $-40\%$), and proportional to contamination
confidence. For the masking example above ($T{=}0.59$, $P{=}0.9$),
$d(0.9){=}0.733$ gives $T_{\text{final}}{=}0.43<\tau$, correctly flagging the
response. A secondary modifier mildly reduces $T$ when the best retrieval
similarity falls below $0.30$ (low-confidence retrieval). The final score maps
to four trust levels (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW/VERY~LOW); the binary decision uses
$\tau{=}0.5$.

\textbf{LLM dependency.} Because $S_{\text{factuality}}$ is an NLI entailment
score against the generated answer, it depends on \emph{generation style}, not
only content. Hedged prefixes (e.g., ``\emph{Based on the provided
context\dots}'') lower entailment and thus $T$, even for correct answers. This
makes $\tau$ and the weights effectively LLM-specific hyperparameters
(Section~\ref{sec:grid}).

\section{Experimental Setup}
%==============================================================================
\textbf{Datasets.} We use two public benchmarks. \emph{TruthfulQA}
\cite{lin2022truthfulqa} documents are formatted as ``question\,+\,best
answer'' ($\approx$20--30 words); its counterintuitive correct answers make it a
realistic poisoning testbed. \emph{FEVER} \cite{thorne2018fever} documents are
enriched to ``question\,+\,verdict\,+\,claim'' ($\approx$30--40 words); bare
claims of 8--12 words caused $\sim$70\% of factuality scores to fall back to the
inconclusive $0.5$ baseline, motivating the enrichment.

\textbf{Protocol.} Each run has two parts: 50 clean queries (Part~A) and the
same 50 queries against a corpus with 30\% of documents poisoned (Part~B), for
100 samples per run. We evaluate four rule-based poisoning strategies:
\emph{contradiction} (appends a contradicting statement), \emph{instruction
injection} (appends an override directive), \emph{entity swap} (in-place
replacement of entities/numbers, leaving no appended artifacts), and
\emph{subtle manipulation} (false qualifiers/hedging). A \emph{mixed} setting
assigns strategies randomly across poisoned documents.

\textbf{Models and metrics.} Documents are embedded with
\texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} \cite{reimers2019sentence} (384-d) or
\texttt{snowflake-arctic-embed2} (1024-d), indexed in FAISS (cosine, top-$K$).
Generation uses \texttt{llama3.3:70b} (primary) or \texttt{qwen3.5:35b}; NLI
runs on CPU. We report accuracy, precision, recall, F1, and \emph{trust score
separation} $\Delta = \bar{T}_{\text{clean}} - \bar{T}_{\text{poisoned}}$, a
threshold-independent measure of discriminability. The comparison point is a
\emph{naive always-trust} RAG that never flags; under 30\% poisoning its
accuracy ($\approx$85\%) reflects class imbalance, not detection, so $\Delta$
and F1 are the informative metrics. We add 95\% confidence intervals: Wilson
intervals for proportions and a percentile bootstrap ($B{=}20\,000$) for F1.

\section{Results}
%==============================================================================
\subsection{Detection Performance and Per-Strategy Hierarchy}
On the primary TruthfulQA mixed run (Llama~3.3~70B + MiniLM, $K{=}5$) the agent
attains 91\% accuracy (95\% CI 83.8--95.2\%), 100\% precision (zero false
positives), 40\% recall (19.8--64.3\%), F1 57.1\% (25.0--80.0\%), and
$\Delta{=}0.225$---a +7\% accuracy gain over the naive baseline. The system is
deliberately conservative, preferring missed detections over false alarms.

Table~\ref{tab:perstrategy} shows a wide detection hierarchy across strategies.
Instruction injection is essentially solved (100\% recall [79.6--100\%], F1
96.8\%, $\Delta{=}0.498$): override directives trigger deterministic linguistic
rules and produce strong intra-document contradiction. Contradiction is
moderate (53.3\% recall [30.1--75.2\%]). Subtle manipulation is weak (20\%
[7.0--45.2\%]) and entity swap is undetected (0\% [0--20.4\%], $\Delta{=}0.053$):
these in-place edits leave no surface artifacts. Because each run has only 15
poisoned samples, recall CIs are wide; we report them to avoid overstating the
precision of the ranking. The ordering itself, however, is consistent and
mechanistically explained.

\subsection{LLM and Embedding Sensitivity}
\label{sec:grid}
Table~\ref{tab:grid} reports a $2\times2$ factorial study ($K{=}3$). Two findings
stand out. First, \emph{LLM choice dominates}: both Llama configurations are
identical (91\%, 100\% precision) regardless of embedding model, while both Qwen
configurations \emph{underperform the naive baseline} by 14 points (71\%) with
precision collapsing to 25--28\%. The cause is generation style: Qwen's hedged,
verbose answers lower NLI entailment, depressing the mean clean trust score to
$\approx$0.64 (vs.\ $\approx$0.83 for Llama) and producing 21--23 false
positives out of 85 clean samples. Second, embedding dimensionality is
near-irrelevant for a concise-output LLM. These results confirm that $\tau{=}0.5$
is implicitly calibrated for Llama and that \emph{per-LLM calibration} is
required before deploying with a different generator.

\subsection{Robustness: Variance, Threshold-Independence, and Calibration}
\label{sec:robust}

\input{roc_figure.tex}

\textbf{Run-to-run variance.} We re-ran each configuration 2--5 times with
independent generations. Despite LLM sampling stochasticity, detection metrics
are highly stable (Table~\ref{tab:variance}): the primary mixed configuration
scores $90.6{\pm}0.5\%$ accuracy and $56.1{\pm}1.3\%$ F1 over five repeats, and
injection is invariant at $99.0{\pm}0.0\%$. The low variance indicates that the
agent's verdicts are driven by the retrieved evidence rather than by the surface
wording of any single generation.

\textbf{Threshold-independent performance.} The fixed $\tau{=}0.5$ operating
point understates the Trust Index. Pooling the mixed-strategy runs, it attains
ROC-AUC $0.81$ (Llama), $0.79$ (Mistral~7B), and $0.73$ (Qwen)
(Fig.~\ref{fig:roc}). All three sit well above chance, so Qwen's weak accuracy
at $\tau{=}0.5$ is a \emph{thresholding} artifact, not an absence of signal.

\textbf{Per-LLM calibration.} We also add a third LLM, Mistral~7B~Instruct, which reaches $87\%$ accuracy---far
above Qwen~3.5~35B ($69\%$) despite being five times smaller. This indicates the
Trust Index's performance is governed by generation \emph{style} (Qwen's hedged
phrasing depresses NLI entailment) rather than model scale. Fitting $\tau$ on
clean scores only (10th percentile of a held-out clean split) and evaluating on
disjoint clean and poisoned samples (Table~\ref{tab:calib}) restores
baseline-competitive accuracy---e.g.\ Qwen rises from $65.5\%$ to $74.5\%$ by
cutting false positives. Crucially, the three LLMs require three \emph{different}
optimal thresholds ($\tau^\star{=}0.71$, $0.58$, $0.43$ for Llama, Mistral, and
Qwen), directly confirming that $\tau$ is an LLM-specific hyperparameter.
Absolute accuracies in Table~\ref{tab:calib} are lower than in
Table~\ref{tab:variance} because the calibration split is deliberately
poison-enriched; the meaningful comparison is before vs.\ after calibration on
the same split.

\subsection{Retrieval Depth and Overhead}
Increasing retrieval depth from $K{=}3$ to $K{=}5$ leaves the mixed-strategy
outcome \emph{identical} (91\% accuracy, 100\% precision, 40\% recall, F1
57.1\%; $\Delta$ changes $0.240\!\to\!0.225$, within noise). The undetected
contexts are entity-swap and subtle attacks that leave no textual signal at any
depth, so additional documents add no poison evidence. Detection is thus
\emph{retrieval-depth-invariant}, and the 40\% recall ceiling is set by attack
difficulty, not retrieval breadth; $K{=}3$ is preferable, achieving the same
result at roughly half the NLI cost ($\sim$9 vs.\ $\sim$20 calls per batch).

The evaluation adds $\approx$14.7\,s per sample ($\approx$17.2\,s end to end),
an $\approx$17$\times$ overhead over baseline RAG, dominated by up to 20 CPU NLI
forward passes at $K{=}5$. This suits high-stakes batch settings (compliance,
document verification, medical review) rather than interactive use; GPU
inference is projected to cut evaluation below 2\,s.

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Generalization}
On FEVER, the full 100-sample run reaches 73\% accuracy and \emph{underperforms}
the 85\% baseline by 12 points, with separation collapsing to $\Delta{=}0.035$.
Although enrichment supplies adequate NLI context, the clean/poisoned trust
distributions overlap, indicating that the Trust Index does not generalize
out-of-the-box and requires dataset-specific calibration. We report this
negative result explicitly; it bounds the external validity of the TruthfulQA
findings and motivates the per-LLM/per-domain calibration discussed below.

%==============================================================================
\section{Discussion}
%==============================================================================
\textbf{An architectural limit, not a tuning failure.} Entity-swap and subtle
attacks change facts in place without textual artifacts. No amount of threshold
tuning, reweighting, or deeper retrieval recovers them (Sections V-A, V-C);
detecting them requires external world knowledge (e.g., verifying an
entity--attribute pair against a structured KB). The 40\% mixed-strategy recall
ceiling is therefore intrinsic to surface-signal detection.

\textbf{Class imbalance makes accuracy misleading.} A do-nothing baseline scores
85\% under 30\% poisoning. The agent's value is better seen in 100\% precision
and the per-strategy separation range (0.053--0.498), which indicates which
attack families are deployable under the current design and which need new
mechanisms.

\textbf{False-positive spillover.} A clean query that retrieves a poisoned
neighbor receives elevated poison probability. This is security-correct (the
environment is contaminated) but costs precision; relevance-weighted aggregation
mitigates but does not eliminate it when a poisoned document ranks highly.

\textbf{Threats to validity.} Attacks are rule-based rather than
gradient-optimized; sample sizes are modest (15 poisoned per run, hence wide
recall CIs); results depend on a specific cluster/model stack; and ground-truth
labels are index-aligned rather than retrieval-grounded. The FEVER result shows
these caveats matter for generalization.

%==============================================================================
\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
%==============================================================================
We presented an autonomous Evaluation Agent that adds an interpretable trust
layer to RAG by unifying NLI-based factual verification, multi-signal poison
detection, and a composite Trust Index with a non-linear dampener. It detects
overt poisoning reliably (injection 100\% recall, 91\% mixed accuracy, 100\%
precision on TruthfulQA), while we identify and explain a hard limit against
in-place edits, demonstrate that NLI-based trust is LLM-dependent, and show
detection is retrieval-depth-invariant. The honest FEVER result bounds
generalization.

Priorities for future work are: (i)~evaluation against gradient-optimized
collision attacks \cite{zou2024poisoned}; (ii)~per-LLM Trust Index calibration
(fitting $\tau$ to the clean-score distribution); (iii)~semantic world-knowledge
integration (e.g., Wikidata lookups) to close the entity-swap gap;
(iv)~GPU-accelerated, streaming evaluation for real-time use; and (v)~true
attack-success-rate measurement of misinformation \emph{adoption}, not just
detection. The framework, adversarial dataset generator, and serialized
experiments are released to support reproducibility.
